ILC for eMNS

Bachelor thesis. Learning-based control for a clinical electromagnetic navigation system

Bachelor thesis (RWTH Aachen), carried out at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems (Learning and Dynamical Systems group) and the Multi-Scale Robotics Lab at ETH Zürich. The thesis received the highest grade. The paper is shared first authorship with Jasan Zughaibi.

Paper (arXiv) · Video · Code (ROS package)

Electromagnetic navigation systems (eMNS) steer magnetic devices such as catheters inside the body without contact. They are usually controlled quasi-statically, close to equilibrium. I worked on dynamic trajectory tracking far from equilibrium, using a magnetically actuated inverted pendulum on the clinical Navion eMNS as the test case. The controller works with torques and forces, so the same approach applies to other magnetic devices.

The method combines:

  • trajectory optimisation that accounts for the internal dynamics of the eMNS,
  • time-varying LQR feedback around the optimised trajectory,
  • iterative learning control (ILC), which uses data from previous trials and the system model to correct the feedforward command.

After each trial, an iteration-domain Kalman filter updates the disturbance estimate, and the next feedforward input is computed by solving a constrained convex optimisation problem. Once the pendulum is close to upright, a separate LQR controller balances it.

Control architecture: trajectory tracking with ILC feedforward and time-varying LQR feedback, switching to a balancing controller near the upright position.

LQR alone did not achieve the swing-up because of effects in the magnetic actuation that the model does not capture. With ILC, the pendulum swung up within six iterations. The correction learned by ILC closely matched the torque error predicted by a high-fidelity calibration of the magnetic field model.
